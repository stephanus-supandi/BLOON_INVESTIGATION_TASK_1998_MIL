# Evidence Grading

Grades describe how directly the available source supports a **specific claim**. A grade is not a measure of how plausible a story sounds.

- **A — Direct, location-specific evidence:** a contemporaneous record, original document, or traceable firsthand testimony that directly supports the event, place, and time in question. Record limitations and corroboration needs.
- **B — Credible supporting evidence:** a reliable historical source supports the claim but is indirect, retrospective, or incomplete on one important detail.
- **C — Area-level context:** a source establishes broader conditions or events in a district/corridor but does not establish the specific business, building, person, or route.
- **D — Modern observation or unverified hypothesis:** present-day Street View, a current business listing, geographic proximity, inference from business type, or an unsourced assertion. Useful for generating questions; not proof of a 1998 event.
- **U — Unassessed:** source not yet obtained or checked.

## Rules

1. Grade each claim separately; do not assign a single grade to an entire narrative.
2. A source may support one part of a claim but not another. Record the exact supported part.
3. Preserve source wording accurately and provide page/section or timestamp where possible.
4. Separate what the source says from the researcher's interpretation.
5. Conflicting sources must remain visible in the register until reconciled.
6. Do not infer ethnicity, ownership, motive, participation, or command responsibility from a business name, cuisine, religion, road layout, or current location alone.
7. Do not mark a claim verified merely because it is consistent with a broad report of unrest.
8. Keep status labels explicit: `NOT_CHECKED`, `SUPPORTED`, `CONTRADICTED`, `PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED`, or `UNRESOLVED`.
