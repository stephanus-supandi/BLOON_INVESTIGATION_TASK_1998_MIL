# Source and Provenance Policy

## Minimum record for each source

Record, where available:

- Author, institution, or witness identifier
- Full title
- Publication or creation date
- Source type (primary document, contemporaneous reporting, retrospective study, interview, modern imagery, etc.)
- Publisher/archive and stable URL or archive identifier
- Page, section, paragraph, or timestamp supporting the claim
- Date accessed
- Relevant quotation or faithful summary
- Limitations, conflicts, and corroboration needed

## Source handling

- Prefer original reports, official records, contemporaneous reporting, and traceable testimony when relevant.
- A source being official does not make every statement in it independently proven; identify whether it records an allegation, finding, recommendation, or judgment.
- Distinguish the TGPF report's documented findings and recommendations from later interpretations and from court judgments.
- Avoid treating repeated claims that trace back to one source as independent corroboration.
- Do not fabricate page numbers, quotations, links, dates, or citations.
- Preserve contradictory evidence instead of silently discarding it.

## Modern imagery

For Street View or other present-day imagery, record the image capture date if available, road/location identification, visible feature, and uncertainty. Use it to document present geography or formulate historical questions—not to assert that a business, owner, building, or event existed in the same form in 1998.

## Privacy and safety

Do not publish unnecessary private personal data. Identify private individuals only where relevant, well-sourced, and appropriate to the public-interest research purpose.
